# Skeptic review: claim 2026-11-24-1 (Spiegelung / Dutarte 3-rank law)

## 1. REPRODUCED?
(i) Table rows. Own C code (rank3.c, drive.py): for every fundamental d, 3-rank r of Q(sqrt d) (narrow class group = cycles of reduced indefinite forms, Cohen 5.4.7 composition + rho reduction; 3^r = #{cycles with f^3 principal}, only attempted when 3 | h+) and r' of the mirror disc (-3d, or -d/3 if 3|d; positive-definite reduced forms). Validation: h(-23)=3,h(-47)=5,h(-239)=15,h(-3299)=27 with r=2, real 229/316/321 r=1, 32009 r=2, 401 h=5; every count is a power of 3 (else abort); r'-r in {0,1} for ALL ~1.3 million d tested (Scholz verified, 0 violations); mirror disc always fundamental.
Result: rows X=1000 ... 3,162,278 (36 rows: k=30..65) are IDENTICAL in all 8 columns to table.csv (mine.csv). No row differs. Rows k=66..90 (X>3.16e6) NOT recomputed (my method ~ X^1.5; 10^6 took 58 s, 3.16e6 several minutes; run stopped by time/sandbox stalls). So the claimed 10^9 values (303,963,559; 89,388,474 etc.) rest on the table's internal evidence plus total count check: 303,963,559 ~ 3/pi^2 * 1e9 = 303,963,551 (consistent), and the early rows' exactness.
(ii) Test (b), reimplemented independently (stat.py) from the description: aggregate p_inf=0.297772 se 0.000139 chi2 13.1; r=0 0.333524/0.000155/15.2; r=1 0.111633/0.000240/14.1; r=2 0.036982/0.001111/15.2, 28 dof. Identical to the claim; z = 0.87, 1.23, 2.18, -0.05; all CONSISTENT as claimed.
(iii) Constants: with Cohen-Lenstra-Martinet real-quadratic 3-rank distribution P(r)=3^{-r(r+1)}eta_inf/(eta_r eta_{r+1}) (eta_k=prod_{i<=k}(1-3^-i)), sum_r P(r) 3^-(r+1) = 0.29765117292... ; matches. Also P(s)(1-3^-(s+1))+P(s-1)3^-s reproduces exactly the imaginary CL distribution (0.5601, 0.4201, 0.01969...), so the law is internally consistent. Fouvry-Klueners (msp.org/ant/2010/4-5/ant-v4-n5-p01-p.pdf) read: Conjecture 1.1 (Dutarte 1984), constant "0.29765117...", "tested on a computer by Dutarte [1984, 4.2]... constants are close, but not too close"; no tables. Belabas (mean3-99.pdf) Conj 3.2 states the same 3^-(a+1).

Robustness of (b) (my addition): the verdict depends on the exponent chosen. Same bins, same fit, exponent e in x^-e:
 e=0.10: all four INCONSISTENT (p_inf aggregate 0.3007, z>10); 0.125 INCONSISTENT (0.29922); 0.15 3 of 4 INCONSISTENT; 0.1667 all CONSISTENT; 0.20 INCONSISTENT (0.29704, 0.3324, 0.1098); 0.25, 0.3 INCONSISTENT; 1/3 INCONSISTENT; 1/2 VOID. Chi-square is nearly flat (13-16 for e in 0.10..0.20), so the data cannot select the exponent; the p_inf shift between e=1/6 and 1/8 is 0.0015 = 10 standard errors. Quoted standard errors are conditional on the model being exactly right.

## 3. HONEST?
Disclosure (c) is genuine and prominent: it admits the same one-term model fails (r=1, 4.2 sigma) at 10^10, and limits the claim to 10^9. Good. But: the "CONSISTENT" label at 10^9 is then of little evidential weight: the pre-set test with fixed exponent 1/6 (and 3 sigma) passes at 10^9 and fails at 10^10 on the same data prefix, the r=1 z-score at 10^9 is already 2.18, and my exponent scan shows pass/fail flips for small changes of the exponent with no chi-square penalty. The statement "the x^-1/6 term accounts for the gap" is an assertion about a fit, not evidence; a numerical test cannot confirm a conjectured limit, only fail to reject it under a model. The 10^10 rows are withheld, so (c) cannot be checked. Claim (d) is unverified (see 2). I could only verify table rows up to 3.16e6 (about 36 of 61), not the top range that carries the 10^9 conclusion.
