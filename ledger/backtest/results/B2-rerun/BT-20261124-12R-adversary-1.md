## Restatement, in my own words

**Headline layer.** Is the differentiated state of an adult cell held in place by a small number of trans-acting transcription factors — few enough that supplying them is enough to send the cell all the way back to an embryo-like pluripotent state?

**Operational layer (the canonical question, as reformulated at Gate 1).** Take a named list of twelve embryo/ES-associated regulators. Try every combination of one to six of them (2,509 combinations). Deliver each combination by virus or episome into intact adult cells that are certified mature (lineage-restricted phenotype; an irreversible clonal genomic mark — in the worked case a completed V(D)J rearrangement in a mature peripheral-blood lymphocyte, which also tags the donor; and a control arm that throws no colonies). Forbid every route that brings an egg or another pluripotent cell to the party: no oocyte or egg cytoplasm, no nuclear transfer, no cytoplast transfer, no fusion, hybrid or heterokaryon, no fusion-permitting co-culture, no egg or ES extract. Ask whether any combination yields a clone that (a) passages ≥20 times in ES/LIF with normal karyotype, (b) carries the donor's sequenced V(D)J mark, (c) silences the lymphoid programme and transcribes endogenous *Oct4* and *Rex1* on a vector-discriminating assay from coding-sequence-only vectors, and (d) makes all three germ layers and the germ line in a chimaera — YES on one such clone against zero in an equal-sized empty-vector arm with per-arm clone counts; NO only if all 2,509 combinations, each in ≥10^6 certified donor cells, yield nothing past partial dedifferentiation; everything else is an "inconclusive run".
