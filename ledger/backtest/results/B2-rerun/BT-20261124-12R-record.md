## Motivating gap

Two pre-as-of bodies of work bracket the question and neither answers it. **(1) Oocyte-dependent reprogramming.** Somatic-cell nuclear transfer (Wilmut et al., *Nature* 1997, 385:810–813; Wakayama et al., *Nature* 1998, 394:369–374) shows an adult somatic genome can be returned to totipotency, but only inside oocyte cytoplasm: it establishes nuclear *competence* while saying nothing about what *suffices*. **(2) Dominant-cytoplasm / hybrid reprogramming.** Pluripotent × somatic cell hybrids (Miller & Ruddle, *Cell* 1976, 9(1):45–55) and embryonic-germ-cell × thymocyte hybrids (Tada et al., *EMBO J* 1997, 16(21):6510–6520) show a pluripotent partner can impose its programme on a somatic nucleus, again without isolating the agents, and with the whole pluripotent cell present. **From the other side**, Davis, Weintraub & Lassar (*Cell* 1987, 51:987–1000) showed one transcription factor converting fibroblasts to myoblasts — lineage conversion *inside* the soma, never upward to pluripotency — and Niwa, Miyazaki & Smith (*Nature Genetics* 2000, 24:372–376) showed that Oct-3/4 action is quantitative, raising it in ES cells driving differentiation rather than reinforcing the state, which is a specific reason to doubt the single-factor route.

## What would count as an answer

**YES:** recovery of at least one clone from a factor-receiving arm that (a) is serially passaged ≥20 continuous passages in standard ES/LIF conditions with undiminished clonogenicity and normal karyotype; (b) carries the donor cell's clonal genomic mark — the specific V(D)J rearrangement of the donor lymphocyte, sequenced — proving descent from a cell that was terminally differentiated and not from a contaminating progenitor; (c) silences the donor lineage programme and transcribes endogenous *Oct4* and *Rex1* from its own alleles, measured by a vector-discriminating assay against a parallel ES line, with coding-sequence-only vectors; (d) contributes to all three germ layers and transmits through the germ line in a chimaera — with the empty-vector arm of equal donor-cell number yielding zero clones passing (a)–(d), and both clone counts reported, so that the YES is evidence about the factors.

**Transgene dispensability is deliberately NOT required.** Requiring it was a loophole in the superseded version; a YES here is a claim about sufficiency to *reach* the state, not about the state's subsequent independence of the vector.

**NO:** every one of the 2,509 subsets of size 1–6 (my own arithmetic check: C(12,1..6) = 12+66+220+495+792+924 = 2,509 — correct), each delivered into ≥10^6 donor cells meeting criteria (i)–(iii), yields no clone passing beyond partial dedifferentiation (proliferation, morphology change or marker gain without germ-layer and germ-line competence), with per-subset delivered-cell and clone counts reported. **A NO is a bounded NO** about this enumerated list at combination size ≤6, and the record must say so.

**Anything else** — partial clones only, a clone failing the clonal-mark check, or a factor arm not separated from its empty-vector arm by clone count — is an inconclusive run, not an answer.

## Gate 3 — Significance

**Consequence — if YES.** A single clone meeting (a)–(d) would establish that the differentiated state is held by a small set of trans-acting regulators rather than by irreversible genomic or chromatin change, and that the oocyte is an *efficient* route to reprogramming rather than a *necessary* one. Three things change the next day. First, the mechanistic account of cell identity: a completed V(D)J rearrangement, the field's canonical irreversible somatic mark, would be shown compatible with founding a germ line, which removes the strongest structural argument for one-way differentiation. Second, nuclear-transfer cloning, hybrid/heterokaryon reprogramming and single-factor lineage conversion (the MyoD type of result) would collapse into one mechanism — dominant trans-acting regulators at different points on one potency landscape — where they had been three separate phenomena. Third, patient-matched pluripotent cells from adult tissue become reachable without eggs, nuclear transfer or embryos, which is a capability currently treated as out of reach and which carries the whole ethical and supply bottleneck of the field with it.

**Consequence — if NO.** A bounded NO (this list, size ≤6, 10^6 cells per subset, counts reported) would show that reprogramming to pluripotency needs something the somatic cell cannot supply from its own nucleus: the full oocyte or embryonic cytoplasmic context, or non-transcription-factor activities — replication-coupled demethylation, large-scale chromatin remodelling, histone variant exchange, or a maternal protein store. That would *locate* the missing activity as cytoplasmic and non-sequence-specific, which is a positive finding dressed as a negative, and it would redirect the whole therapeutic programme to nuclear transfer and embryo-derived lines rather than leaving the factor route as an untested hope. It would also validate the pessimistic reading of the dose-dependence result: that pluripotency factors are poised, dose-critical maintenance proteins and not switches.

**Audience, and what they would do differently.** (1) ES-cell and developmental biologists (Smith, Niwa, Schöler, Surani, Blau circles): a YES turns every ES-enriched gene list into a reprogramming screen candidate list, and the next grant written is a combinatorial screen rather than another cytoplasm-transfer experiment. (2) Nuclear-transfer and cloning groups (Wilmut, Wakayama, Yanagimachi, Jaenisch circles): a YES gives them a non-oocyte control arm they currently lack and a way to ask which of the egg's activities is doing the work; a NO tells them the oocyte activity is the object of study and should be fractionated. (3) Regenerative-medicine and transplantation clinicians: a YES moves patient-specific cell replacement from a route requiring human oocytes — scarce, and ethically and legally constrained in most jurisdictions as of the as-of date — to a route requiring a blood draw and a vector. (4) Immunologists: criterion (iii) makes the V(D)J locus a reporter of developmental history, a use it has not had.

**Fecundity — what becomes askable.** If YES: what is the minimal subset, and is minimality cell-type dependent? Which of the factors acts on which somatic mark — are any of the twelve acting as demethylation or remodelling recruiters rather than as classical activators? Does the same logic run *between* somatic lineages, i.e. is there a factor set for any ordered pair of differentiated states? What is the rate-limiting step that makes the frequency low, and is it stochastic chromatin opening or a deterministic barrier? Are imprints and X-inactivation reset, and if not, does the clone still make a germ line? If NO: which oocyte fraction restores competence when added back — a direct fractionation programme that the NO makes compulsory rather than optional.

**Anchoring.** It touches (i) the open question of what the donor nuclei in successful adult cloning actually were — differentiated or rare undifferentiated cells, which criterion (iii) decides; (ii) the dose-dependence anomaly, that a pluripotency factor's overexpression *differentiates* ES cells, which a YES would have to be reconciled with and which is therefore a live mechanistic puzzle either way; (iii) the hybrid-reprogramming literature's unresolved question of what the dominant partner supplies; (iv) the one-way-differentiation assumption behind the textbook account of development.

**Significance score: 3/3.** Argued above, not asserted: named communities, named next experiments on each branch, named anomalies.

## Reason for silence

Four mechanisms, none of them "no one thought of it":

1. **Instrument and inventory gap.** The pluripotency gene set was both small and very recently assembled as of the as-of date — the ES/EC-restricted factors on this list were characterised across roughly 1989–2000 — so a combinatorial screen over them only became formulable at the end of that window. Simultaneous delivery of up to six transgenes into primary cells was not routine, and retroviral vectors of the era require target-cell division, which a mature resting lymphocyte barely does. The experiment was close to unperformable with the vectors on the bench, which is also the candidate's own presupposition 7.
2. **Disciplinary boundary and attentional pull.** After 1997 the entire reprogramming programme — people, funding, journals, public attention — organised itself around the oocyte and nuclear transfer. The agent question ("which molecules?") sat in molecular genetics while the phenomenon sat in embryology, and the method that would answer it, gain-of-function combinatorial screening, belonged to a third community. My query 12, aimed at that third community's vocabulary, returned nothing pre-as-of.
3. **A theoretical reason to expect failure, which is the strongest mechanism here.** The dose-dependence result made the leading candidate factor look like a poised maintenance protein whose overexpression *differentiates* rather than reverts. A field that believes more Oct4 pushes cells out of the pluripotent state has a principled reason not to try pushing more Oct4 into a somatic one. Combined with the fusion literature's lesson that reprogramming needed a whole dominant cell, the prior probability the community assigned to a factor-only route was low enough that the experiment would have read as naive rather than bold — and an experiment that looks naive is unfundable in a way an experiment that looks hard is not.
4. **Cost asymmetry of the NO arm.** The question as posed is cheap to answer YES and brutally expensive to answer NO (2,509 arms × 10^6 qualifying cells). A field rationally avoids designs whose negative result requires nine orders of magnitude of cells, and the pre-registered-exhaustive form is a late habit.

**Credible.** I note the ledger's own check: if no credible reason for silence existed, the novelty finding would itself be suspect and Gate 2 would have to be re-run. Mechanism 3 is the load-bearing one and is the kind of reason — a principled expectation of failure — that this ledger accepts.
