## 1. Restatement in my own words (both layers)

**Headline (plain):** can a few transcription factors, acting on their own, drive a fully differentiated adult cell back to something equivalent to an embryonic pluripotent cell?

**Operational test (canonical, as it now stands, i.e. Gate 1's reformulation included):** fix a list of twelve embryo/ES-enriched regulators (Oct4, Sox2, Rex1, FoxD3, UTF1, Hesx1, Gbx2, Esrrb, Cdx2, Eomes, c-Myc, constitutively active Stat3). Take a mature peripheral-blood lymphocyte that carries a completed V(D)J rearrangement (a mature phenotype, an empty-vector arm making zero colonies, and an irreversible clonal mark that also tags donor identity). With the nucleus never leaving its own cytoplasm, and with egg cytoplasm, nuclear transfer, cytoplast transfer, fusion, hybrids, heterokaryons, fusion-permitting co-culture and egg/ES extracts all forbidden at every step, deliver each of the 2,509 subsets of size 1–6 (my own arithmetic: 12+66+220+495+792+924 = 2,509 — confirmed) by coding-sequence-only viral or episomal vector. **YES** = at least one clone that survives ≥20 continuous ES/LIF passages (~50 doublings) with undiminished clonogenicity and a normal karyotype, transcribes endogenous *Oct4* and *Rex1* on a vector-discriminating assay at bona-fide-ES levels, has switched off the lymphoid programme, carries the donor's sequenced V(D)J mark, and makes all three germ layers plus the germ line in a chimaera — against zero such clones from an equal-sized empty-vector arm, counts reported per arm. **NO** = all 2,509 subsets, each on ≥10⁶ qualifying donor cells, give nothing past partial dedifferentiation. Anything else = inconclusive.

In one line: *is a small defined set of nuclear factors sufficient, with the egg and every fusion route removed, to reset a provably mature mammalian cell all the way to germ-line-competent pluripotency — and if so, how few factors?*
