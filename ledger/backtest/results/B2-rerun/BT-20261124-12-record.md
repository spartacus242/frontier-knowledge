## Question (headline)
Can a handful of transcription factors reprogram a fully differentiated adult cell back into an embryonic-like pluripotent cell? (carried verbatim from the ranker)

## Question (canonical) — version 1, as submitted
If a small defined set (say, under about ten) of sequence-specific transcription factors known to be enriched in early embryonic or germline-derived pluripotent cells is force-expressed in a terminally differentiated adult somatic cell with an unmodified nucleus left in place, does any resulting clone acquire the full pluripotent phenotype — self-renewal in culture, silencing of the donor cell's differentiation programme, re-activation of the endogenous pluripotency loci, and contribution to all three germ layers plus germ line in a chimaera — where recovery of such clones carrying the donor cell's genetic marker counts as yes and failure of all clones to pass beyond partial dedifferentiation (proliferation or marker gain without germ-layer competence) counts as no?

## Question (canonical) — version 2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation (well-posedness only)
Take as donor a clonally derived cell that is terminally differentiated by a pre-declared criterion AND carries a pre-declared heritable mark of somatic origin — either (i) an irreversible somatic genomic rearrangement present in the donor and absent from the germ line (a mature B or T lymphocyte carrying a rearranged immunoglobulin or T-cell-receptor locus), or (ii) a heritable genetic label installed under the control of a terminal-differentiation promoter in a non-lymphoid cell. Force-express, by stable integration, combinations of **at most ten** sequence-specific transcription factors drawn from the rule-generated candidate list below, singly and in combination. Does any clone arising from such a donor satisfy all five of: (a) self-renewal beyond ~20 passages; (b) the donor somatic mark, demonstrating somatic and not resident-stem-cell origin; (c) transcription of the endogenous pluripotency loci from their own promoters, with the introduced transgenes dispensable for maintenance; (d) loss of the donor differentiation programme; (e) derivatives of ectoderm, mesoderm and endoderm in a teratoma **and** chimaera contribution including germ line? Recovery of one such clone counts as YES. Failure of every tested combination, with the combinations, expression levels and clone counts stated, counts as a NO bounded by exactly that list.
**What the repair itself presupposes** (tested as hard as the clause it replaced): that a cell carrying an irreversible rearrangement or a terminal-promoter label can be cultured, stably transduced and clonally expanded at all. For the lymphocyte arm this is the weaker leg — primary terminally differentiated lymphocytes are short-lived in culture and poorly transducible — which is why the marking scheme is written as a disjunction rather than requiring the lymphocyte. Option (ii) carries its own presupposition that the terminal-differentiation promoter is not re-activated or leaky in the pluripotent state, which the record flags as UNVERIFIED rather than assumed.

## Alternative formulations
1. Is the pluripotent state a trans-acting, factor-encoded state rather than a developmentally privileged one — i.e. can overexpression of a short list of embryonic transcription factors alone, with no oocyte cytoplasm, no nuclear transfer and no cell fusion, convert a lineage-marked adult cell into a cell that passes a germ-layer/germ-line competence test?
2. Starting from an adult cell whose differentiated identity is genetically marked, does any combination of a few embryonic transcription factors suffice to erase that identity and install the embryonic-like pluripotent programme, as judged by teratoma or chimaera contribution to all three germ layers, or is the barrier insurmountable by transcription factors alone?

## Significance — consequence
If YES: patient-matched pluripotent cells become obtainable from adult tissue without eggs, nuclear transfer or embryos, and the differentiated state is shown to be held by a small set of trans-acting regulators rather than by irreversible genomic or chromatin change — which would place nuclear-transfer cloning, fusion reprogramming and single-factor lineage conversion under one mechanism. If NO (bounded): reprogramming to pluripotency is shown, over the combinations tested, to need more than a transcription-factor list — the oocyte or embryonic cytoplasmic context, chromatin-remodelling machinery, maternal RNAs or replication-coupled erasure — so therapeutic cell replacement stays tied to nuclear transfer and embryo-derived lines, and the hard distinction between master-regulator switching among neighbouring somatic lineages and reversal of the whole developmental programme survives.

## Significance — audience
Nuclear-transfer and cloning laboratories (who would gain or lose a defined substitute for the oocyte); ES-cell and early-embryo gene-regulation groups (Oct4-class factor labs), who would redirect from necessity-by-deletion to sufficiency-by-combination; lineage-conversion groups working in the MyoD/Pax5 idiom, who would learn whether their idiom extends past neighbouring lineages; and transplantation and disease-modelling groups, for whom a YES removes the embryo from the route to patient-matched cells.

## Significance — fecundity
A YES makes askable: which member of the set does what; whether the same logic converts between any two somatic fates; what the rate-limiting step is and why efficiency is low; whether the erased epigenome is complete or residually somatic. A NO makes askable: which non-transcription-factor activity is the missing one, and whether it is suppliable in extract.

## Significance — anchoring
Touches the pre-2001 anomaly of nuclear-transfer inefficiency with donor-cell specialisation (Prior A's framing), the lymphocyte-rearrangement question about whether fully differentiated nuclei are reprogrammable at all, and the open question of what the egg does that no defined mixture yet does.

## Ambition: 3/3 (ranker's score, carried unchanged; this reader does not rescore)
If yes: it brings within reach a capability now treated as out of reach — making patient-matched pluripotent cells from adult tissue without eggs, nuclear transfer or embryos — and it establishes a possibility result that the differentiated state is held by a small set of trans-acting regulators rather than by irreversible genomic or chromatin change, which would reorganise developmental biology's account of cell identity, open a line of work on directed fate conversion and disease modelling from patient cells, and unify nuclear-transfer cloning, cell-fusion reprogramming and single-factor lineage conversion (the MyoD type of result) under one mechanism.
If no: reprogramming to pluripotency would be shown to need the full oocyte or embryonic-cell cytoplasmic context (many factors, or non-transcription-factor activities such as chromatin remodelling or replication-coupled erasure), so therapeutic cell replacement would remain tied to nuclear transfer and embryo-derived lines, and the textbook picture of differentiation would keep a hard distinction between master-regulator switching among neighbouring somatic lineages and reversal of the whole developmental programme.

## Reason for silence
A disciplinary boundary plus a method cost, not "nobody thought of it". The erasing activity was owned by nuclear-transfer embryology and framed as a property of egg cytoplasm — Prior A, opened at abstract level, attributes it to molecular chaperones and enzymatic activities of the oocyte, egg or zygote — so the natural next experiment in that field was a better egg or a better donor nucleus, not a factor list. The gain-of-function TF idiom was owned by a different community working on adjacent somatic lineages (MyoD, Pax5), whose successes were all within-soma and gave no reason to expect whole-programme reversal. Add the method cost: multi-gene stable delivery in combinations, a selectable readout for endogenous pluripotency-locus reactivation, and chimaera/germ-line scoring were each expensive pre-2001, and the combinatorial space the rule generates is large, so the experiment reads as an unbounded screen rather than a decisive test. (These are mechanisms I can name; the first is sourced to Prior A's abstract, the second and third are **my own reasoning about the state of the methods, not a source finding** — the search that would have grounded them, a pre-2001 sweep on multi-gene retroviral delivery efficiency and Oct4-locus reporters, was not run.)
