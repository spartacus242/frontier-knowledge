## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE. Is there such a thing as a lone single sheet of graphite that holds together as a crystal, and are the charge carriers in it massless, light-like particles?

OPERATIONAL TEST (canonical). Take a carbon sheet exactly one atom thick, sp2-bonded, obtained by detaching it from bulk graphite, either hanging free or lying on an insulator it does not bond to chemically. LIMB ONE: does it keep honeycomb lattice order over at least 1 micrometre for at least a day at room temperature (no, if such sheets always roll up, crumple, melt or break up below that size)? LIMB TWO: on such a sheet, do the carriers show a linear energy-momentum relation, with a cyclotron mass going to zero as the square root of density and a field-independent Landau level at the band-touching energy (no, if a finite band-edge mass or a gap above resolution is seen)? Joint yes needs both. The record's P-k says any ONE of three signatures suffices, while the canonical sentence joins them with "and".
