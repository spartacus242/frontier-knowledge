## Restatement (mine, written before any attack)

HEADLINE. Is one carbon plane of graphite a material in its own right - does it persist as an ordered sheet with no bulk crystal bonded beneath it - and do its conduction electrons follow a zero-rest-mass, light-like energy-momentum law?

OPERATIONAL TEST. Take a sheet of sp2 carbon exactly one atom thick, separated from graphite, either hanging free or lying on an insulator it is not chemically bonded to. Limb one: does it stay a honeycomb lattice over at least 1 micrometre for at least a day near 300 K, scored per specimen on five items (one layer by two independent methods; lateral extent; lattice order by diffraction or imaging; support state; persistence)? NO if such sheets always scroll, crumple, melt or fragment below that size. Limb two, only on a sheet that passed limb one: do its carriers show a massless two-dimensional spectrum, by any one of three pre-declared signatures (linear E(k) by a momentum-resolved probe; cyclotron mass proportional to the square root of density and extrapolating to zero; Landau levels spaced as the square root of field times index with one level fixed at the band-touching energy)? NO if a finite band-edge mass or a gap beyond resolution appears.

The canonical sentence joins the three signatures with "and"; the record says its P-k ("any one") governs. I attack the question as it stands with that precedence rule, and test the rule itself under attack 4.
