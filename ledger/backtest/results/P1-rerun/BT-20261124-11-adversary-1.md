# BT-20261124-11 - Gate 4 adversary #1 (BACKTEST, as-of 1998-01-01)

## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE: Is one lone carbon plane of graphite a stable crystal when nothing but itself (or mere contact) holds it, and are its conduction electrons massless, light-like quasiparticles?

OPERATIONAL TEST: Take a sheet of sp2 carbon exactly one atom thick, off its parent crystal, hanging free or lying on an insulator it does not bond to. (Limb one) Does it keep honeycomb lattice order over at least 1 micrometre for at least a day at about 300 K - or does every such sheet scroll, crumple, melt or break up below that size? (Limb two) On such a sheet, do the carriers show a Dirac-cone spectrum, tested by linear E(k) from a momentum-resolved probe, a cyclotron mass going as the square root of density and extrapolating to zero, and a field-independent Landau level at the band-touching energy - or a finite band-edge mass or gap? The canonical sentence joins the three signatures with "and"; the record's P-k and answer criterion accept "any one".
