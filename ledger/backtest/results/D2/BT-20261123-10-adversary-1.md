## Restatement, in my own words

**Headline layer (as the ranker fixed it):** can taking the brake off a T cell — by antibody-blocking one inhibitory receptor on the T cell itself — unleash enough anti-tumour immunity to destroy a tumour that is already growing?

**Operational layer (version 2, the one permitted Gate 1 repair, which is the wording I attack):** take immunocompetent syngeneic mice; grow a *named* transplantable tumour line that has been pre-qualified in a control arm of the same colony as weakly immunogenic (≤10% spontaneous rejection, ≤10% rejection after immunisation with irradiated cells of that line); wait until tumours are palpable at ≥5 mm mean diameter; only then start systemic anti-CTLA-4 monoclonal antibody, against an isotype-matched control antibody. Count hosts with **no palpable tumour for ≥60 days**. YES = ≥30% of treated hosts versus ≤5% of controls, with the effect abolished by combined CD4/CD8 depletion, survivors resisting rechallenge with the same line, and receptor occupancy documented on T cells including in draining lymphoid tissue. NO = growth and tumour-free survival indistinguishable from control despite documented occupancy and a positive internal control showing the antibody amplifies T-cell responses in the same animals. 10–29% complete regression is a pre-declared PARTIAL; death from autoimmunity before the endpoint is pre-declared UNINTERPRETABLE.

**Note on layer asymmetry (my own reasoning, not a source finding):** the headline is existential ("can"), the operational test is about one qualified line. A YES in one line answers the headline; a NO in one line does not. I raise this under attacks 5 and 8, not as a depth problem.
