Question (headline): Can blocking an inhibitory receptor on T cells release an immune response strong enough to reject established tumours?
Question (canonical): In immunocompetent syngeneic mice bearing palpable established tumours of ≥5 mm mean diameter of a named weakly immunogenic transplantable line — the line declared before treatment and qualified in a pre-run control arm of the same colony as showing ≤10% spontaneous rejection and ≤10% rejection after immunisation with irradiated cells of that line — does systemic anti-CTLA-4 monoclonal antibody, begun only after that size is reached, versus an isotype-matched control antibody, produce complete regression with no palpable tumour for ≥60 days in ≥30% of treated hosts against ≤5% in control, with the effect abolished by combined CD4/CD8 depletion, with survivors resisting rechallenge with the same line, and under documented receptor occupancy on T cells including draining lymphoid tissue?
Reformulation note: this is version 2, the one permitted Gate 1 repair; version 1 said "substantial fraction", "pre-declared durable interval" and "weakly immunogenic line" without enumerating any of the three. The repair states a rule (≤10%/≤10%) rather than a list and fixes two numbers; its own presupposition that a line meets that rule in the colony used is UNVERIFIED.
Alternative formulations: (a) Does antibody interruption of a negative signalling receptor on T cells, given after tumour establishment, convert progressive growth of a poorly immunogenic syngeneic tumour into T-cell-dependent complete regression? (b) Is the ceiling on anti-tumour T-cell responses in a tumour-bearing host set by an inhibitory receptor on the T cell itself — testable by whether blocking that receptor alone, with no vaccine, cytokine or transfer, eradicates an already-growing tumour?
Motivating gap: pre-as-of print located the failure of anti-tumour immunity on the tumour side or in insufficient costimulation and tried to fix it by adding signal (B7 transfection, cytokine gene transfer, vaccines, adoptive transfer). The untested inversion is subtraction at the T cell itself, in a host whose tumour is already established, with nothing added. Krummel & Allison, J Exp Med 1995;182(2):459–465 (full text, opened by the record and independently by two adversaries) contains no occurrence of tumor/tumour/antitumor/immunotherapy/therapy; its only in-vivo clause records CTLA-4-Ig *reducing* immune responses.
Depth (object test; deletion test): Object — the answer would be a fact about tumour-bearing immunocompetent mice, their T cells and the established tumour tissue in them; one row = one tumour-bearing animal. Deletion — if every paper, registry entry and protocol on tumour immunology vanished tonight, the mice would still fix the answer: the rejection either happens in those animals or it does not; the antibody is the exposure, and the effect is in the world. depth_pass = true in the record and Pass in all three adversary logs, so no Gate 0 rejection.

MEASURAND EXEMPTION declared and applied by all three adversaries: the question pre-declares both "blockade does nothing" (NO) and autoimmune death (UNINTERPRETABLE), so neither is attackable as a false premise.
What would count as an answer: YES — ≥30% of blockade-treated hosts in complete regression and tumour-free ≥60 days against ≤5% of isotype controls, growth curves separated, effect abolished by CD4/CD8 depletion, survivors resisting rechallenge, occupancy documented. NO — with documented occupancy and a positive internal control showing the antibody amplifies T-cell responses in the same animals, growth and tumour-free survival statistically indistinguishable from control. PARTIAL — growth delay without clearance, or clearance only in small tumours or only with a co-administered vaccine or cytokine (10–29% complete regression is pre-declared PARTIAL, not a NO). UNINTERPRETABLE — death from autoimmunity or antibody toxicity before the tumour endpoint.
