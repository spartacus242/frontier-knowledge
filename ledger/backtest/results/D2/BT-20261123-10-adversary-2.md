## Restatement (mine, before any attack)

**Headline, in my words:** if you plug a receptor that is thought to damp a T cell's own activation, and you do it in an animal whose tumour is already growing, will that animal's own lymphocytes destroy the tumour? The question is about sufficiency — not whether the brake exists, but whether taking it off is by itself enough.

**Operational test as it now stands (version 2, the repair — I judge this wording, not version 1):** take immunocompetent syngeneic mice; pick, in advance, one transplantable tumour line that a pre-run arm in the same colony shows is poorly seen by the host (≤10% rejection on its own, ≤10% rejection even after immunising with irradiated cells of that line); let tumours grow to ≥5 mm mean diameter; then give systemic anti-CTLA-4 mAb or an isotype control; count the hosts in which the tumour disappears completely and stays gone ≥60 days (≥30% treated vs ≤5% control); show the effect disappears when CD4 and CD8 cells are depleted, show survivors reject a rechallenge of the same line, and document that the antibody actually occupied receptor on T cells, including in draining lymphoid tissue. 10–29% complete regression is pre-declared PARTIAL; death from autoimmunity before the endpoint is pre-declared UNINTERPRETABLE.
