Question (headline): Can a material with a negative refractive index be built, and could it focus light more finely than the diffraction limit allows?
Question (canonical): For a passive composite of sub-wavelength conducting elements whose effective permittivity and permeability are both negative in a common frequency band, does (i) a wedge of it refract a beam to the same side of the surface normal as the incident beam, with a transmission passband appearing only where both responses are negative, and (ii) a plane-parallel slab of it form, of a source much smaller than the free-space wavelength, an image outside the slab whose intensity full width at half maximum is below half the free-space wavelength when the same source at the same source-to-detector distance with the slab removed gives a width above it - yes if both hold at any electromagnetic frequency, no if (i) fails in every such structure or if (i) holds but (ii) is excluded by measurement or by solving Maxwell's equations for the slab?
(No Gate 1 reformulation was used. Pre-declared by rule: threshold half the free-space wavelength; source extent at most one tenth of it; lattice period below one fifth of it.)
Alternative formulations: (1) For a point dipole in front of a homogeneous slab with relative permittivity and permeability both minus one plus a small declared loss, do Fourier components with transverse wavenumber above the free-space wavenumber reach the image plane with larger amplitude than over the same path in vacuum, and does any buildable wire-and-loop lattice reproduce that slab closely enough for the measured image width to fall below half a wavelength? (2) Does a manufacturable medium exist with antiparallel phase velocity and energy flow over an isotropic band (shown by a prism), and does a flat lens of it resolve two incoherent point sources closer than half the free-space wavelength where they are unresolved without it?
