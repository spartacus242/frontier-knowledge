## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE: is it physically possible to make a medium that bends waves the "wrong way" (refractive index below zero), and would a flat lens of such a medium beat the usual half-wavelength limit on how small a focal spot can be?

OPERATIONAL TEST: take a passive artificial medium built from metal elements much smaller than the wavelength, arranged so that its bulk electric response and its bulk magnetic response are both negative over one band. (i) Does a prism of it send a beam out on the same side of the normal as it came in on, and does it transmit only because both responses are negative (take out either set of elements and transmission dies)? (ii) Does a flat slab of it turn the field of a very small source into a spot, outside the slab, whose intensity width at half height is under half a free-space wavelength, where the field at the same place with the slab taken away is wider than that? YES if both are seen at some frequency. NO if no such prism ever refracts that way (or theory forbids it), or it does but the slab spot is shown, by measurement or by solving Maxwell's equations, not to get under half a wavelength.
