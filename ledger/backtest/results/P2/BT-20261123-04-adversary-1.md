## Restatement (my own words)

Headline layer: Is it possible to make a material that bends electromagnetic waves the "wrong" way (a negative refractive index), and would a lens made of it produce a focus sharper than the diffraction limit?

Operational layer: Take a passive lattice of metal elements, each much smaller than the wavelength, engineered so that its bulk electric and magnetic responses are both negative over one shared frequency band. Two things are then tested. (i) Does a prism cut from it send a beam out on the same side of the normal as it came in, with the lattice transmitting only because both responses are negative (kill either element set and the band goes dark)? (ii) Does a flat slab of it produce, outside the slab, an image of a far-sub-wavelength source whose intensity FWHM is under half the free-space wavelength, when the same source at the same total distance without the slab gives more than half a wavelength? YES needs both, at any electromagnetic frequency. NO is either "(i) fails in every such structure" or "(i) holds and (ii) is excluded by measurement or by a Maxwell solution for the slab".
