## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE: Is there a way to make a material whose refractive index is negative, and if so would a lens made of it concentrate light into a spot smaller than diffraction permits?

OPERATIONAL TEST: Take a passive lattice of sub-wavelength metal elements for which the homogenised permittivity and permeability are both negative over one shared band. (i) Cut a prism from it: does the transmitted beam leave on the same side of the exit-face normal as the internal beam arrives, and does the band transmit only where both homogenised constants are negative? (ii) Cut a flat plate from it: for a source far smaller than the wavelength, is the intensity pattern in the image plane behind the plate narrower than half the free-space wavelength, when the same source at the same distance in empty space gives a pattern wider than that? YES needs both at some frequency. NO needs (i) to fail for every such structure, or (i) to hold while (ii) is ruled out by measurement or by a Maxwell solution for the plate.

The two layers are not the same size: the headline says "light" and "material", the test accepts any frequency and only conductor composites. The record flags the first mismatch (presupposition 9) and not the second.
