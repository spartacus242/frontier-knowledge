## Restatement (my words)

Headline: is there any way to make someone else sure that a claim is true without handing them anything beyond the bare truth of the claim?

Operational test (canonical): take an NP-complete membership statement (graph 3-colourability). Does a two-party randomised conversation exist in which (a) an honest prover always gets a polynomial-time randomised verifier to accept a true statement, (b) no prover, however it behaves, gets a false one accepted except with negligible probability, and (c) any polynomial-time party that talks to the prover on a true instance ends up able to compute nothing it could not have computed from the instance plus the fact of membership? YES is a definition of (c), a protocol and a proof (unconditional or under an encryption-style hardness assumption). NO is a theorem that any language with such a conversation is already in probabilistic polynomial time.

The two layers differ in one thing that matters below: the headline says "a statement", the test (record's P7) narrows it to a statement about a COMMON input whose truth is fixed independently of what either party claims, with a privately held certificate.
