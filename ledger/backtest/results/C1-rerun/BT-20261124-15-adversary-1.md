## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE: Is there a way for one party to leave a second party justifiably sure that some claim is true, such that the second party ends up no better off in any other respect than if it had simply been told, by an oracle it trusts, "the claim is true"?

OPERATIONAL TEST: Fix an NP-complete language (3-colourability of graphs). Does a two-party randomised conversation exist in which (a) the party who answers can always get a polynomial-time randomised checker to accept a 3-colourable graph, (b) no answering strategy gets a non-3-colourable graph accepted except with negligible probability, and (c) whatever polynomial-time randomised strategy the checker follows, there is nothing it can compute after the conversation that it could not compute from the graph plus the single bit "it is 3-colourable"? A yes is a definition of (c), a protocol and a proof (unconditional or resting on a named hardness assumption of the kind used for encryption). A no is a theorem that any language with such a conversation is already decidable in randomised polynomial time.
