## 1. Restatement in my own words

**Headline.** Can a sceptic be made certain that a claim is true without coming away with anything else - no part of the reason it is true, no usable by-product - beyond the single fact that it is true?

**Operational test (canonical).** Fix one NP-complete language, graph 3-colourability. Is there a randomised conversation between a prover and a randomised polynomial-time verifier such that (a) an honest prover always gets a 3-colourable graph accepted, (b) on a graph that is not 3-colourable no prover strategy at all gets acceptance except with negligible probability, and (c) whatever randomised polynomial-time strategy the verifying side follows, anything it can compute after talking to the prover about a 3-colourable graph it could already compute, up to negligible advantage, from the graph and the promise that it is 3-colourable? YES = a definition, a protocol and a proof, unconditional or under a named hardness assumption of the kind used for encryption. NO = a theorem that every language having such a protocol is decidable in probabilistic polynomial time. Honest-verifier-only results and results for intermediate languages only (quadratic residuosity, graph isomorphism) are labelled partial.

No Gate 1 reformulation was used, so there is no repair clause to test separately.

## 6. The step from Yao to the headline, written out and labelled

EVERY LINE OF THIS SECTION IS MY OWN INFERENCE. Nothing here is printed in any document I opened, and Theorems 2 and 4 are printed without proof, so the rule-6 exception (a derivation carried out in full from results the prior prints with proof) does NOT apply.

Let j be a graph and i an assignment of three colours to its vertices, and let f(i, j) = 1 exactly when i is a proper colouring of j. f is a 0-1 function with a circuit of size linear in the number of edges (my arithmetic: one inequality test per edge and one conjunction). Theorem 4 as printed then asserts a protocol computing f, of cost O(C(f) log 1/εδγ log 1/γ), in which a party who follows the protocol faces successful cheating with probability at most γ. By Definition 1, outside that event either Bob detects cheating or Alice's behaviour is consistent with some value i'; then Bob's result is f(i', j), which is 0 for every i' when j is not 3-colourable. So Bob obtains 1 on a non-3-colourable graph with probability at most γ, and obtains 1 when Alice holds a proper colouring and both follow the protocol. The privacy constraint leaves Bob's posterior over Alice's colouring within 1+O(ε) of uniform on the set of proper colourings.

Taken at face value, then, Yao's announced theorems give the headline in the sense "the sceptic learns nothing about WHICH reason the prover holds". They do NOT give the operational test, and this is the part that actually blocks the transfer: the printed privacy constraint is about the posterior over the other party's variable. A conversation that also carried a proper colouring drawn independently of i would leave that posterior uniform and so satisfy the constraint as printed, while handing the verifying side something it could not compute from the graph and the bare fact. "Nothing about the other's variable" and "nothing it could not already compute" are different conditions. I ran no search that could ground this distinction in a pre-1983 source; it rests on my reading of the printed definition alone.
