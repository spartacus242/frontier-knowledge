Question (headline): Are chronic gastritis and peptic ulcer disease caused by a bacterial infection of the stomach?

Question (canonical): In adults having upper endoscopy with antral biopsy, is a mucosa-associated bacterium (seen on histology and/or cultured from the biopsy) present in most patients with histological chronic gastritis and with gastric or duodenal ulcer not attributable to anti-inflammatory drugs or a gastrin-secreting tumour, and rare where the mucosa is histologically normal, AND does clearing that organism with an antimicrobial regimen, compared with ulcer healing by acid suppression alone, resolve the gastritis and lower endoscopic ulcer relapse over the following 12 months - yes if both hold, no if the organism is as common on normal mucosa or if clearance changes neither gastritis nor relapse?
(No reformulation made at Gate 1; the one permitted reformulation remains unused. See Gate 1 note.)

Alternative formulations: (1) Does the stomach lining of people with chronic gastritis or peptic ulcer carry a specific bacterium that normal stomach lining does not, and does the disease go away and stay away when, and only when, that bacterium is eliminated? (2) Among ulcer patients randomised after healing to a regimen that eliminates gastric mucosal bacteria versus one that does not, does bacterial clearance (confirmed on repeat biopsy) predict histological resolution of gastritis and freedom from ulcer recurrence, and does acquisition of the organism by a previously normal stomach produce gastritis?
