## Restatement (my words)

**Headline:** Is long-standing inflammation of the stomach lining, and ulceration of the stomach and duodenum, the result of bacteria infecting the stomach?

**Operational test:** Among adults who get an endoscopic antral biopsy, (limb 1) is some bacterium attached to the mucosa found in most people with histological chronic gastritis or with a gastric/duodenal ulcer that is not explained by anti-inflammatory drugs or a gastrinoma, and seldom in people with normal mucosa; and (limb 2) when an antimicrobial course removes that bacterium, do the gastritis heal and the ulcers relapse less over 12 months than when the ulcer is healed by acid suppression alone? YES needs both limbs; NO if the organism is as common on normal mucosa or if clearance changes neither outcome.

## DEPTH CHECK: Pass

"The answer would be a fact about **human gastric and duodenal mucosa, the bacteria living on it, and whether removing them changes inflammation and ulcer recurrence in patients**." Tissue, pathogens and patients are on the pass list. Deletion test: if every paper and protocol vanished, stomachs and their flora would still fix the answer. Rows test: one row is one biopsied or randomised patient. No match to clauses (a)-(e): nothing published is being audited or corrected. A Pass is not a point in the candidate's favour.
