## Restatement (my words)

- Headline: can a party be persuaded that a claim is true by a demonstration that hands over nothing usable beyond the bare truth of the claim?
- Operational test: take an NP language not known to be easy (declared: quadratic residuosity modulo a composite of unknown factorisation; satisfiability). Is there a randomised two-party exchange with a polynomial-time checker that (i) always accepts true membership claims, (ii) accepts a false one only with negligible probability whatever the claimant does, and (iii) leaves the checker able to compute efficiently nothing it could not already compute efficiently from the instance alone, given the claim is true? YES = such an exchange is proved (possibly under a stated hardness assumption) for a declared language; NO = a theorem that any language with such an exchange is easy.
