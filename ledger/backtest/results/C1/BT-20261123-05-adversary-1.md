## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE: Is it possible to make someone certain that a claim is true without them coming away with anything else they did not already have?

OPERATIONAL TEST: Fix a hard-looking NP language (the two named ones: "a is a square modulo a composite N whose factors are not given", and satisfiability). Is there a back-and-forth, coin-tossing conversation between a prover and a polynomial-time checker such that (i) true claims are always accepted, (ii) no prover behaviour gets a false claim accepted except with negligible chance, and (iii) whatever the checker can feasibly compute after the conversation it could feasibly have computed from the claim alone, once told the claim is true. YES = such a protocol is proved for one of the two languages, possibly under a stated hardness assumption. NO = a theorem that only languages decidable in probabilistic polynomial time have such protocols.

## DEPTH CHECK: Pass

"The answer would be a fact about the intrinsic behaviour of a class of computations - two-party randomised interactive protocols between a prover and a polynomially bounded verifier deciding membership in NP languages." On the pass list. Deletion test: if every paper and protocol write-up vanished, whether such a protocol exists is still fixed by mathematics. Rows: a (language, protocol) pair - an instance of the computational object, not a record. Clause check: no match to (a)-(e); settled by a theorem. Variation check: the thing that would have to differ is a mathematical fact; the answer is relative to the chosen formal definition of "reveals nothing", but that is true of every mathematical question and the canonical wording fixes one. A Pass is not a finding in the candidate's favour.

## Three defects in the record, found on the opened Yao text

D1 (clipped quotation, move-away class). The record's sourced leg (i) of its rule 6 transfer argument is: the remedy Yao cites for cheating "will force both parties to reveal their variables". The sentence opens "However," and is the paper's objection to a verification stage; two sentences later the paper asserts "The following results will show that one can thwart cheating, without asking either to reveal the variable." and states Theorem 4. The record cites the paper for the state of affairs the paper says it has left behind. The same clipped string is reused in Significance ("C: force both parties to reveal their variables") as the 1982 status quo a yes-answer would replace.

D2 (own-reasoning leg contradicted by the print). The record's leg (ii), labelled its own reasoning, says Yao's cost is stated in the size N of the input set so a polynomial-time verifier is outside the regime as printed. Theorems 2 and 4 are stated in "a boolean circuit of size C(f)", with T(A) = O(C(f) log 1/(epsilon delta gamma) log 1/gamma). For a witness-checking predicate the circuit is polynomial in the statement length (the last step is my arithmetic-level inference, not a line in Yao).

D3 (anchoring). The record anchors on "the cheating problem left open in C sec. 3.3(B)". As printed, Yao does not leave it open: he announces it solved (Theorem 4), without a printed proof. I did not run a sweep for the record's sec. 6 string "Is there a protocol such that an honest party will not be double-crossed," so I neither confirm nor strike it; what I was served from sec. 6 is an impossibility theorem (Theorem 8), i.e. that question is answered in the paper for secret exchange.
