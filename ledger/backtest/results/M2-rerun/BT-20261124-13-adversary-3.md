## 4. My own reasoning and arithmetic (NONE of this is a source finding; each block says what it uses)

LEMMA A (non-degeneracy; answers P10(iii) in part). Let G be a finite non-abelian simple group whose order has r distinct prime divisors, chi a non-trivial irreducible character, and (H_n) any sequence satisfying v2. For psi != 1 put f_psi(q) = sum_n <H_n, psi> q^n. Then the f_psi span a space of dimension >= r; in particular at least r distinct non-trivial irreducibles occur among the H_n.
 Proof. (1) Theta_g := (j - 744) - T_g = sum_n (H_n(1) - H_n(g)) q^n = sum_{psi != 1} (psi(1) - psi(g)) f_psi, so Theta_g lies in the span F of the f_psi. (2) Theta_g is a weight-zero function on Gamma_0(N_g), holomorphic on H, with expansion O(q). (3) Theta_g != 0 for g != 1: its q-coefficient is chi(1) - chi(g), and chi(g) = chi(1) only on the kernel of chi, which is trivial because G is simple. (4) Take g_i of prime order p_i for each of the r primes (Cauchy). (5) The Theta_{g_i} are linearly independent: if lambda_k Theta_{g_k} = - sum_{i != k} lambda_i Theta_{g_i} =: Fn, then Fn is invariant under Gamma_0(p_k^a) and under Gamma_0(N') with N' prime to p_k. Those two groups generate SL_2(Z) (both contain Gamma(p_k^a N'); modulo it their images are B x SL_2 and SL_2 x B in SL_2(Z/p_k^a) x SL_2(Z/N'), which together generate the product). So Fn is a holomorphic SL_2(Z)-invariant function vanishing at the cusp, hence zero, hence lambda_k = 0. (6) So dim F >= r. QED.
 Uses: orthogonality of characters, kernel of a character, Cauchy's theorem, surjectivity of SL_2(Z) -> SL_2(Z/m), and the fact that a level-one function holomorphic on H and at the cusp is constant. All classical; none opened. With r = 15 (Ogg, conditional on existence) no family confined to fewer than 15 non-trivial irreducibles satisfies v2. NOT shown: that no "cheap" solution with 15 or more exists.

REMARK A2 (why no cheap solution turned up; same ingredients as Lemma A). Write V_p for the weight-zero functions of p-power level, holomorphic on H and vanishing at the cusp; the sum of the V_p over distinct primes is direct (the argument of step 5). Suppose every f_psi were a sum of pieces f_{psi,p} in V_p with NON-NEGATIVE coefficients. For g of order prime to p the V_p-part of Theta_g must vanish, i.e. sum_psi (psi(1) - psi(g)) f_{psi,p} = 0; each term has non-negative real part, so any psi with f_{psi,p} != 0 has every p-regular element in its kernel, and those elements generate G (G simple, not a p-group), so psi = 1. Hence no such solution exists: any admissible sequence needs multiplicity series whose prime-level parts cancel against each other across different primes, or parts of composite level. This is why the one-level trick behind v1 has no direct many-prime analogue. It is NOT a proof that every solution is "natural".

LEMMA B (the test has no finite evidence layer). For every prime p and every finite list of integers t_1..t_K there is a weight-zero modular function on Gamma_0(p), holomorphic on H, with expansion q^-1 + t_1 q + ... + t_K q^K + O(q^(K+1)).
 Proof. u_p := Delta(p tau)/Delta(tau) = q^(p-1) prod ((1-q^(pn))/(1-q^n))^24 is on Gamma_0(p), holomorphic and non-vanishing on H, with integer coefficients and leading coefficient 1. For k >= 1 put a = ceil(k/(p-1)), m = a(p-1) - k >= 0 and v_k := u_p^a j^m = q^k + O(q^(k+1)), integral. Then (j - 744) + sum_{k<=K} a_k v_k matches any integer prefix by a triangular solve over Z. QED. (Checked numerically for p = 3: u_3 j = q + 768 q^2 + ...; for p = 2, u_2 = h below.)
