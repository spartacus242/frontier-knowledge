## Attack 1 - working (ALL OF THIS IS MY OWN REASONING AND ARITHMETIC, NOT A SOURCE FINDING; no document was opened for any step)

Record's v1 construction, re-checked by my arithmetic: 196884 x 24 = 4,725,216; 196884 x 276 = 54,339,984; 196884 x 2048 = 403,218,432 - all three agree with the record. c(2), c(3), c(4) are from memory, as in the record.

A lemma that extends the record's exclusion (proof in full). Suppose an admissible sequence lies in the non-negative span of irreducibles theta_0 = 1, theta_1 = chi, theta_2, ..., theta_r: H_n = sum_j m_j(n) theta_j. Write m_j(tau) = sum_n m_j(n) q^n, putting the 1/q term in m_0. Then T_g = sum_j theta_j(g) m_j(tau), and H_1 = 1 + chi gives m_1 = q + O(q^2). Let M(p) be the set of weight-zero functions holomorphic on H and invariant under Gamma_0(p^a) for some a; it is a vector space because Gamma_0(p^b) is contained in Gamma_0(p^a) for b >= a. For g of p-power order (including g = 1) v2 puts T_g in M(p). If the vectors (theta_0(g), ..., theta_r(g)), g of p-power order, span Q^(r+1), then each m_j is a rational combination of those T_g, so m_j is in M(p). If that holds for two primes p != p', m_1 is invariant under Gamma_0(p^a) and Gamma_0(p'^b). Those two groups generate SL_2(Z): both contain T = (1 1; 0 1); they contain L^(p^a) and L^(p'^b) for L = (1 0; 1 1); gcd(p^a, p'^b) = 1 gives L; and T, L generate SL_2(Z). So m_1 is a level-one weight-zero function, holomorphic on H, with expansion q + O(q^2): holomorphic at the cusp and vanishing there, hence identically zero on the compact curve - contradicting the coefficient 1. So no admissible sequence exists in such a span. For r = 1 this is the record's argument (the vectors (1, chi(1)) and (1, chi(g)) span Q^2 because chi is faithful).
What the lemma does NOT do: it says nothing when the spanning condition fails at all but one prime, and I opened no character table, so I cannot say for which sets of characters it holds. It narrows P10(iii); it does not close it.

The family I tried to complete. Order the fifteen primes p_1 < ... < p_15 and let S_i = {p_1..p_i}. Let A_S(g) = chi(g_{S'}), the value of chi on the S'-part of g. If chi is rational-valued (an extra assumption I did not source) A_S is a virtual character, being chi composed with a power map. Put B_i = A_{S_i} - A_{S_(i-1)}: it vanishes unless p_i divides ord(g), and the B_i sum to chi(1) - chi. For each prime p take an integral f_p = q + O(q^2) of weight zero on Gamma_0(p), holomorphic on H: (eta(p tau)/eta(tau))^(24/(p-1)) for p in {2,3,5,7,13}, and f^2 E_4^2 / Delta with f an integral weight-2 cusp form on Gamma_0(p) with leading term q for the others (existence recalled, not opened; for p = 11, f = eta(tau)^2 eta(11 tau)^2). Then T_g = (j - 744) - sum_i B_i(g) f_{p_i} has T_1 = j - 744, q-coefficient 1 + chi(g), H_n(1) = c(n), is holomorphic on H, and is modular on Gamma_0 of the product of the primes dividing ord(g). Every clause of v2 is met EXCEPT that H_n must be a genuine character. It is not automatically one: H_n = (c(n) - 196883 f_{15,n}).1 + f_{1,n} chi + sum_{i=1..14} (f_{i+1,n} - f_{i,n}) A_{S_i}, and the A_{S_i} are virtual in general (for the 2-dimensional character of S_3 and p = 2, g -> chi(g_{2'}) is 1 - sign + chi). There is a structural reason too: in a simple group, a non-zero virtual character B that vanishes on every p-regular element has a non-trivial constituent of positive multiplicity (otherwise B = m.1 - Psi with Psi genuine and Psi(g) = Psi(1) on all p-regular g, forcing Psi trivial and B = 0), so positivity would have to come from cancellation between primes. I could not decide whether that cancellation is possible. I therefore have NOT produced a degenerate sequence satisfying v2, and I do not claim one exists.
