## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE: When the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are looked at on the scale of their own mean gap, are the gaps between them distributed the way the gaps between eigenvalues of a large random matrix are?

OPERATIONAL TEST: List the ordinates of the nontrivial zeros in increasing order, stretch the axis so that there is asymptotically one zero per unit length, and form two empirical objects from the first N zeros: (i) the density of differences between pairs of distinct stretched zeros, and (ii) the distribution of differences between neighbours. Ask whether, as N grows without bound, BOTH converge, and whether the two limits are the two-point function and the neighbour-gap law of ONE of the three Dyson classes (orthogonal, unitary, symplectic). YES only if both limits exist and belong to the same named class; NO if either limit does not exist or is anything else, Poisson included.

Both layers ask the same thing; the operational test is a faithful sharpening of the headline (it narrows "random matrices" to the three Dyson classes and "statistics" to two named statistics).
