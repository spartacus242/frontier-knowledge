## Restatement (my own words)

HEADLINE: Do the gaps between the zeros of the Riemann zeta function behave, statistically, like the gaps between eigenvalues of big random matrices?

OPERATIONAL TEST: List the heights of the nontrivial zeta zeros in increasing order, stretch the height axis so that zeros arrive at (asymptotically) one per unit length, and look at two statistics over the first N zeros as N grows without bound: (i) the density of differences between all pairs of stretched zeros, and (ii) the histogram of differences between neighbouring stretched zeros. YES if both settle to the two-point cluster function and neighbour-spacing law of the same one of Dyson's three ensembles (orthogonal, unitary, symplectic); NO if either fails to settle, or settles to anything else (Poisson, a law without repulsion, or a repulsive law that is none of the three).

Two things I note in restating, before any attack. (a) The test is conjunctive and its target set is three named laws, while the headline says "large random matrices" without restriction: a YES on the test is a YES on the headline, but a NO on the test is not a NO on the headline. (b) The stretching rule written into the canonical sentence gives unit mean spacing only in the limit (record's own arithmetic, which I re-derived below).
