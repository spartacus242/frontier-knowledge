Question (headline): Do the spacings between zeros of the Riemann zeta function follow the same statistics as the eigenvalues of large random matrices?

Question (canonical): For the ordinates 0 < g_1 <= g_2 <= ... of the nontrivial zeros of the Riemann zeta function, rescaled to unit mean spacing by w_n = (g_n / 2pi) * log(g_n / 2pi), do the pair-correlation density of the w_n and the distribution of the consecutive gaps w_(n+1) - w_n over n <= N converge as N -> infinity to the two-level cluster function and nearest-neighbour spacing law of one of Dyson's three matrix ensembles (orthogonal, unitary or symplectic, in their large-dimension limit) - YES if both limits exist and equal those of one named ensemble, NO if a limit fails to exist or equals anything else (in particular the Poisson law 1 and exp(-s), or a law with no level repulsion at zero separation)?

Reason for silence: (my own reasoning, not a source finding) The two literatures had no shared readership or vocabulary. The random-matrix laws were printed in physics journals and tied by their authors to physical symmetry conditions (confirmed in Dyson I), so nothing in them invites an arithmetic application. Number theory's habitual statistics for zeros were the counting function, S(T) and extreme gaps; "unfold to unit mean spacing and histogram the gaps" is a spectroscopist's reflex, not a number theorist's. And the large zero computations were designed to verify the Riemann hypothesis by sign counts, so they did not print the ordinates a gap histogram needs.
