## Restatement (my words)

- Headline layer: once the zeros of the Riemann zeta function are stretched so that one zero falls per unit length on average, is their local arrangement statistically the same as the local arrangement of eigenvalues of very large random matrices?
- Operational test (canonical question): list the positive ordinates of the nontrivial zeros in order, rescale by w_n = (g_n/2pi) log(g_n/2pi), and form two statistics over the first N zeros: the density of differences w_m - w_n between distinct zeros, and the empirical distribution of consecutive gaps. Let N grow. YES if both statistics converge and both limits are those of ONE of Dyson's three circular ensembles (orthogonal, unitary, symplectic) at large dimension. NO if either limit fails to exist or is anything else, Poisson included.
- The operational test is narrower than the headline in one respect the record flags (only three ensembles count as "random matrices") and is a statement about a limit, so only a proof decides it; numerics can only be a partial answer.

HINDSIGHT (one line): hardest to set aside was knowing that the conjecture posed months after the as-of date names the unitary ensemble and is still unproved; the first made one pruning argument look obvious (I carry it below as an unverified lead and build nothing on it), the second tempted me to grade Triviality on later history instead of on a first-hand check.
