## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE: Is a bacterium that lives in the human stomach the cause of chronic gastritis and of peptic ulcer?

OPERATIONAL TEST: Among adults having endoscopy (aspirin/anti-inflammatory users and gastrinoma excluded), three things are checked and reported separately for chronic gastritis, duodenal ulcer and gastric ulcer. (i) On blinded histology and culture of antral and body biopsies, are mucosal bacteria found in a clearly larger share of diseased than of histologically normal stomachs? (ii) Does a pure culture of them, given to a host with biopsy-proven normal mucosa, produce histological gastritis? (iii) In a randomised comparison against acid suppression alone, does an antibacterial regimen that is shown to clear them on repeat biopsy make the gastritis resolve and lower 12-month endoscopic ulcer relapse? YES needs (i) and (iii), with (ii) or a documented reason it cannot be done; NO is failure of (i) under adequate method or of (iii) despite clearance; (i) alone is COLONISER-ONLY.

Is the headline itself already asked or answered? ASKED, yes, and the record concedes it (headline novelty Low). I verified the raising source first-hand (below). Whether it was ANSWERED before 1983 is the open point of this log and I could not close it.
