Question (headline): Are chronic gastritis and peptic ulcer disease caused by a bacterial infection of the stomach?
Question (canonical): In adults having upper endoscopy (excluding regular aspirin/anti-inflammatory users and gastrin-secreting tumour), are bacteria on or in the gastric mucosa (i) present, on blinded histology and culture of antral and body biopsies, in a clearly larger fraction of patients with histological chronic gastritis, duodenal ulcer or gastric ulcer than of patients with histologically normal mucosa, (ii) able in pure culture to produce histological gastritis when given to a host with previously normal mucosa, and (iii) such that randomised antibacterial treatment which clears them on repeat biopsy resolves the gastritis and lowers 12-month endoscopic ulcer relapse compared with acid-lowering treatment alone - YES if all three hold, NO if blinded biopsies show no association or clearance changes neither gastritis nor relapse, and COLONISER-ONLY if (i) holds while (ii) and (iii) fail?
(No reformulation was used at Gate 1.)
Alternative formulations:
1. Do the three classical lines of causal evidence - consistent presence in diseased but not normal human gastric mucosa, reproduction of gastritis by a pure culture in a previously normal stomach, and cure of gastritis with a fall in ulcer recurrence after the organisms are eliminated - all hold for a bacterium living in the human stomach?
2. If the bacteria some pathologists have seen on inflamed gastric mucosa are removed from ulcer patients by antibacterial drugs in a randomised comparison with acid suppression alone, does the gastritis heal and the ulcer stay healed at one year, and does a pure culture of those bacteria inflame a normal stomach?
Motivating gap: Organisms were repeatedly reported on human gastric mucosa (Celler and Thalhimer 1916, "constant presence of streptococci"; Freedberg and Barron 1940; Steer 1975, "Bacteria have been found related to the gastric epithelium") with causation expressly left undetermined, while an in-window review (Strickland and Mackay 1973, abstract) offers only "several mucosal \"irritants\"" for the common Type B gastritis.
