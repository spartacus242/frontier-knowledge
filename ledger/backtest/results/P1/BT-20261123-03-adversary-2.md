## Restatement (my words)

HEADLINE (plain layer): Can one atomic layer of graphite be had as a self-standing, stable crystal, and are the charge carriers in it massless, Dirac-like particles?

OPERATIONAL TEST (canonical layer): Take an sp2 carbon sheet certified to be exactly one atom thick, held either over a hole (arm A) or on a chemically inert insulator (arm B), with no chemical bond and no epitaxial registry to anything. (1) Does a probe that can count layers show it to be a hexagonal crystal over micrometres at room temperature, still there after days? (2) Do its carriers show the massless-linear-band signature set, which the record enumerates as three members: cyclotron mass going as sqrt(carrier density); Landau levels going as sqrt(B) with a field-independent zero-energy level; a half-period shift in magneto-oscillation phase and Hall plateau sequence. YES needs both. NO is: no such sheet persists, OR finite density-independent mass / a gap / conventional phase and plateaus.

The two layers are logically separable: the first conjunct is an existence-and-stability question about a material, the second a band-structure question conditional on the first.
