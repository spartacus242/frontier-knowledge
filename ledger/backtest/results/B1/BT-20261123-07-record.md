Question (headline): Do the clustered regularly interspaced repeats found in many bacterial and archaeal genomes defend the cell against foreign DNA such as viruses?

Question (canonical, as submitted — VERSION 1): In a transformable, phage-susceptible prokaryote carrying a clustered interspaced repeat array together with its adjacent cas genes (for concreteness Escherichia coli K-12 with its iap-proximal array, Streptococcus thermophilus, Haloferax/Sulfolobus for the archaeal side), does loss of the array or of the cas genes raise the efficiency of plaquing of a challenge phage, or the transformation/retention frequency of a plasmid, whose sequence matches one of the resident spacers — and does introducing into a naive isogenic host a single new spacer matching a phage it is otherwise sensitive to lower that phage's efficiency of plaquing — with yes meaning a spacer-sequence-specific, cas-dependent reduction in foreign-DNA establishment of at least an order of magnitude relative to the isogenic control, and no meaning that deletion and spacer addition leave plaquing and plasmid establishment statistically indistinguishable from control while the same strains remain sensitive to the phage and competent for the plasmid?

Question (canonical — VERSION 2, the one permitted Gate 1 reformulation, for well-posedness only): In one or more of the enumerated hosts — Escherichia coli K-12, Streptococcus thermophilus, Haloferax volcanii, Sulfolobus solfataricus — the PRIMARY limb is the construction limb and does not depend on any resident spacer matching an obtainable invader: in an isogenic trio consisting of (a) wild type, (b) a host whose repeat array has had one spacer replaced by a 30–40 nt sequence taken from a phage or plasmid the host is demonstrably permissive to, and (c) the same engineered host additionally deleted for the adjacent cas genes, does the engineered spacer lower that invader's efficiency of plaquing (or plasmid transformation/retention frequency) by >= 1 order of magnitude relative to (a), with the reduction abolished in (c) and absent for a sequence-unmatched control invader? The SECONDARY limb, runnable only where presupposition P3 happens to hold, is the deletion limb of version 1: whole-array and cas deletions tested against an invader matching a resident spacer. YES requires the primary limb's ordered inequality (a) ~ (c) >> (b) on the matched invader with no effect on the unmatched one; NO is all establishment frequencies within error across the trio and across >= 2 invaders, in strains shown by control to be sensitive and competent.
Reason for the reformulation: P3 (that some resident spacer matches an invader obtainable and assayable on that host) is, pre-2005, not merely contested but unestablished — no spacer-versus-mobile-element sequence comparison is in print — and version 1 made the whole question hostage to it.

Significance — consequence: If YES, prokaryotes have a genome-encoded, sequence-specific defence system alongside restriction–modification, receptor variation and abortive infection — the first one whose specificity is written in a heritable, variable sequence record rather than in a protein's recognition site. Models of phage–host coevolution acquire a host arm that can change specificity without changing a protein; barriers to horizontal gene transfer, including plasmid and resistance-plasmid spread, acquire a sequence-dependent term; and the unexplained phage resistance of industrial fermentation strains acquires a candidate mechanism with a predictive handle (spacer content). If NO, a locus present in a large share of bacterial and most archaeal genomes stays functionless, the competing accounts (replicon partitioning, DNA repair, thermophile genome maintenance) remain live, and the field learns that the spacers' variability is not about invaders — which is itself informative, because it makes the spacer-as-barcode typing practice safe to read as neutral drift.
Significance — audience: (i) phage biologists and dairy/industrial microbiologists, who would immediately start typing spacer content in phage-resistant derivatives and stop attributing all unexplained resistance to adsorption and restriction; (ii) comparative genomicists and annotators, who would re-annotate the cas family away from repair; (iii) molecular epidemiologists using spoligotyping and spacer typing, whose marker would turn out to be under selection by phage exposure rather than neutral, which changes how the typing data may be read phylogenetically; (iv) archaeal virologists, who gain a host-side locus to perturb.

Reason for silence: the arrays entered the literature twice, and both entrances pointed away from this question. They were first described as a feature of a gene's flanking structure (the 1987 iap paper) and then as a strain-typing substrate, a tradition in which the spacer is a barcode — valuable precisely because its sequence is arbitrary and uninformative — so the people with the most spacer sequence in hand had a professional reason to treat spacer content as meaningless. Second, the conserved protein partners were annotated by motif as helicase and exonuclease, which in the 2002 annotation culture reads immediately as repair; a sequence-based prediction of that kind is self-sufficient and generates no challenge experiment, so the hypothesis closed the question rather than opening it. Third, the two bodies of knowledge needed to ask the candidate's question sat in non-overlapping communities: comparative genomicists and molecular typists held the repeat and spacer data, while phage physiologists and dairy microbiologists held the plaque assays and the phage stocks, and neither had a reason to delete a locus of unknown function in a sequenced thermophile. Fourth, the obvious cheap test of the hypothesis — compare spacers to phage genomes — was only becoming possible as phage genome sequence accumulated, and until it was done nobody had a reason to incur the cost of array deletion. Fifth, the prevailing frame for antiviral defence was protein recognition of a site, so a defence whose specificity is stored as a heritable sequence record had no slot to occupy in the field's taxonomy of mechanisms.
