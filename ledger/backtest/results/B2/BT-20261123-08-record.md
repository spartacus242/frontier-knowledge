Question (headline): Can a handful of transcription factors reprogram a fully differentiated adult cell back into an embryonic-like pluripotent cell?

Question (canonical, version 1, as supplied): If a small defined combination (say two to six) of transcription factors selected for enrichment in mouse embryonic stem cells relative to somatic cells is forcibly and continuously expressed in cultured adult mouse somatic cells of a terminally differentiated, clonally traceable lineage — mature B or T lymphocytes carrying an irreversibly rearranged antigen-receptor locus, or lineage-marked post-mitotic-derived fibroblasts — do any resulting clones acquire the full embryonic-stem-cell phenotype (indefinite self-renewal in ES conditions, reactivation of the endogenous Oct4/Nanog loci, extinction of the donor lineage program, teratomas containing all three germ layers, and contribution to chimaeras including germline transmission) while still carrying the donor's somatic genomic rearrangement as proof of descent from the differentiated cell: YES if at least one such clone meets every criterion including the donor-specific rearrangement, NO if, after saturating the tractable factor combinations, no clone progresses past partial or transgene-dependent marker expression without full-phenotype acquisition?

Question (canonical, version 2, ONE Gate 1 reformulation, used hereafter): Does there exist a combination of at most six factors drawn from the pre-declared pool P (defined by rule below) whose continuous forced expression in cultured adult mouse somatic cells carrying a clonal, lineage-irreversible genomic mark (rearranged Igh/Tcrb) yields at least one clonal line satisfying all of (b)–(e) below while PCR-positive for the donor rearrangement — YES on one such line; NO if every subset of P of size ≤ 6 that is deliverable in a single experiment series has been expressed and assayed under Oct4- or Nanog-locus selection and no clone satisfies (b)–(e) together, with the failure point named?
