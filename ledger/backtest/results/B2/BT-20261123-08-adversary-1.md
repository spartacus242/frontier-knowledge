## Restatement, in my own words (both layers)

**Headline, as I read it:** is the differentiated state of an adult mammalian cell reversible by a small number of trans-acting gene products — i.e. can forcing a handful of embryo/ES-specific transcription factors into a fully differentiated adult cell put it back into the embryonic pluripotent state, with no egg and no ES cell involved?

**Operational test (version 2, the repaired wording I judge), as I read it:** take cultured adult mouse cells that carry an irreversible somatic genomic signature (a rearranged Igh or Tcrb locus) so that any derived line can be proved to descend from a differentiated cell; force continuous expression of every subset of size ≤ 6 of a 14-gene pool P that the record itself assembled from the pre-2006 literature; select at an endogenous Oct4 or Nanog locus; and ask whether any one clonal line simultaneously (b) reactivates endogenous Oct4/Nanog with the donor programme off, (c) self-renews indefinitely, (d) makes three-germ-layer teratomas, (e) goes germline in chimaeras, while staying PCR-positive for the donor rearrangement. YES on one such line; NO when every deliverable subset has been run and scored and the failure point is named.

So: the headline asks whether the reprogramming input is low-dimensional; the operational test asks whether a particular enumerated pool, searched to depth six, contains a sufficient subset, with descent from a differentiated cell certified genetically.
