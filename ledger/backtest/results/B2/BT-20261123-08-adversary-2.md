## Restatement, in my own words (both layers)

**Headline, as I read it.** Is the embryonic state recoverable from an adult, already-specialised cell by handing it a few regulatory proteins — i.e. is "being differentiated" held in place by a small set of trans-acting switches rather than by an irreversible history?

**Operational test, version 2 (the one I attack; the repair is in scope).** Take cultured adult mouse somatic cells that carry a clonal, lineage-irreversible genomic signature (a rearranged Igh or Tcrb locus). Force continuous expression of some subset of size ≤ 6 drawn from a pool P of 14 ES-enriched, pluripotency-implicated genes that the record itself enumerated from the pre-2006 literature. Select on an Oct4- or Nanog-locus reporter. YES if one clonal line is PCR-positive for the donor rearrangement AND reactivates endogenous Oct4/Nanog with the donor programme off AND self-renews AND makes three-germ-layer teratomas AND contributes to chimaeras with germline transmission. NO if every deliverable subset of size ≤ 6 has been expressed and assayed and no clone meets (b)–(e) together, with the failure point named.

So: layer 1 asks whether the differentiated state is low-dimensionally reversible; layer 2 is a bounded screen with a descent certificate attached.
