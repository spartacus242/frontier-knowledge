Question (headline): Does a neural network's error fall as a predictable power law in the amount of data and the size of the model, across very different tasks?
Question (canonical): For networks newly trained on four enumerated tasks (image classification, word-level language modelling, machine translation, speech recognition), over a grid of 8 nested training-set sizes spanning at least two decades and 5 model sizes spanning at least 1.5 decades of parameter count, tuned by one pre-declared search per cell with 3 seeds, does held-out error follow eps(n,m) = c + a*n^(-alpha) + b*m^(-beta) well enough that a fit to the lower cells predicts the top-decade cells within 10% relative error and beats logarithmic and exponential forms in every task (yes), or does at least one task fail with a better-fitting alternative form (no)?
(Intake wording unchanged; no Gate 1 reformulation was spent. "Top-decade" misdescribes the enumerated split, which governs: fit on n <= 2^-3 and the 3 smallest widths, predict n = 2^-2, 2^-1, 2^0 and the 2 largest widths.)
Alternative formulations: (1) At geometrically spaced data sizes and parameter counts on vision, language-modelling, translation and speech tasks, are the curves in n and in m straight on log-log axes after subtracting a fitted floor, with exponents from small cells that extrapolate correctly to the largest cells in all four tasks? (2) Is the generalisation error of trained deep networks a separable power-law surface in sample count and parameter count whose form (not necessarily its exponents) is shared across four unrelated task types, judged by out-of-range prediction against pre-declared logarithmic and exponential rivals?
