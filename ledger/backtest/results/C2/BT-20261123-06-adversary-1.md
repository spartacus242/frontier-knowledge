## Restatement (mine, not the record's)

HEADLINE: when a neural network is trained with more data, or made bigger, does its test error come down along a power law that can be forecast, and is that so for unrelated kinds of task?

OPERATIONAL TEST: train new networks on ImageNet-1k (ResNet widths), One Billion Word (LSTM widths), WMT En-De (attention encoder-decoder widths) and LibriSpeech 960 h (recurrent acoustic-model widths), at 8 nested data fractions (2^0..2^-7) and 5 widths (>= 1.5 decades of parameters), 3 seeds, one pre-declared hyperparameter search per cell. Fit eps = c + a n^-alpha + b m^-beta on the cells with n <= 2^-3 and the 3 smallest widths. YES if, in all four tasks, median relative prediction error on the remaining cells is <= 10% AND the power-law surface has lower held-out error than c - a log n - b log m and c + a exp(-n/n0) + b exp(-m/m0). Otherwise NO, naming the failing task and the better form.
